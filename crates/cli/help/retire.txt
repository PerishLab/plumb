Destroy one declared delivery chain in a fixed order.

Retirement is bounded by declaration, never by argument. A product that can be
retired names its bucket and zone under [release.retire]; one that declares
nothing cannot be retired at all, and says so instead of asking.

The dry run is the default and reads no credentials. It prints the target and
the plan, which is the same fixed order --execute follows: inventory, archive
and purge credentials, revoke the writer, detach the domain, empty and delete
the bucket, delete the repository, remove the local escrow.

--execute acts only when --confirm-repo, --confirm-bucket, and --confirm-domain
each equal their target verbatim. A confirmation that merely looks right is
refused by name.

Authority enters through PLUMB_RETIRE_ACCOUNT and PLUMB_RETIRE_TOKEN. Plumb
cuts short-lived scoped tokens from that factory, bounds them with an expiry,
and revokes them in an arm that runs whether the sweep succeeded or failed. A
derived token never enters a command argument or a log, and none outlives the
command that cut it. The factory credential itself is provisioned elsewhere:
Plumb consumes it and never mints one.
